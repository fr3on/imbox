# A letter about email, and who gets to knock.

*Why Imbox exists.*

Hello—

For ten years my mail lived in Office 365. For four more it lived in Google. Fourteen years of mornings that began the same way: open the door, and see who has been waiting.

Both were good tools. They were made for everyone, which is to say for no one in particular. And somewhere in those years I noticed that I had stopped reading my mail and started processing it. A letter is something you receive. A message in a queue is something you clear.

An inbox is a strange kind of mirror. It does not show you what you value. It shows you who knocks the loudest. A stranger with a discount, a system that wants a signature, the person you love: all of them arrive through the same door, in the same type, in the order of the clock. And the clock, it turns out, is a poor judge of what matters.

So I stopped asking how to reach zero, and asked a smaller question. Who should be allowed to reach me, and when?

> A door is not cruelty. A door is how a house stays a home.

That is all Imbox is. A door that waits for your yes. A quiet room for the people you chose. A shelf for the writing you like to read when there is time, and a drawer for the receipts that only need to exist. Nothing in it is clever for its own sake. Every choice is an answer to that one small question.

I built it for myself first, on my own machine, so the mail would sit where I could see the walls. Mail is the closest thing we still have to a handwritten life: addressed, unhurried, kept. It deserves a place that is not rented from someone whose business is your attention.

I do not know if this is the right way to keep a mailbox. I only know that after fourteen years of living inside other people’s answers, I wanted to try my own, and to share it.

If it gives you back even a little silence, it was worth building.

Ahmed
